# Q760 source and priority assessment

**Verdict, 27 November 2026.** The original question concerns the fixed-reference description-gain supremum for subprobabilities under a common σ-finite dominator. The inspected primary literature gives attained, finite-start, or effective-null results. It did not disclose the full arbitrary-reference equality, including infinite gain and classes without a finite-gain anchor. Confidence in this bounded search is moderate.

## Exact source and pagination

Tyron Lardy, Peter Grünwald and Peter Harremoës, *Reverse Information Projections and Optimal E-statistics*, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 70(11), 7616–7631 (2024), DOI 10.1109/TIT.2024.3444458. Equation (1) defines the gain relative to a reference \(Q\), including the total-mass correction. Appendix C, Definitions 3–4, distinguishes finite mixtures, countable mixtures and arbitrary measurable barycenters in the Polish/Borel setting. Its final paragraph conjectures equality for σ-convex \(C\) and its Choquet hull under common σ-finite domination of \(P,Q\) and every member of \(C\). The two accessible CWI PDFs, including the current catalog's 34437 link, both identify as arXiv:2306.16646v3, 30 July 2024. The conjecture is at printed/PDF p. 23 of that manuscript. A page number within the IEEE typeset version was not established. ## Exact overlap and limits

- Lardy, Grünwald and Harremoës, Corollary 1, transfers an already attained probability-measure reverse information projection to stronger convex hulls. It relies on linear e-statistic constraints being invariant under mixtures. Appendix C Lemma 4 proves continuity for normalized partial countable mixtures when a first component has gain greater than negative infinity. Theorem 7 transfers near-optimal sequences from a convex class to its countable hull under the source's zero-minimax-gain premise. Neither is the fixed-reference arbitrary-measurable or \(L^1\)-closure equality in the absence of an anchor. The fixed finite-gain anchor argument given here uses the same standard lower-envelope and Fatou principle as Lemma 4. It is included as a special case, with no separate priority claim. Their Example 4 already uses finite-support laws and a geometric target. The missing-anchor example here takes that construction with a fixed reference \(Q=P\). [Appendix C and Section IV-A](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/34437/34437.pdf#page=21).
- Martin Larsson, Aaditya Ramdas and Johannes Ruf, *The numeraire e-variable and reverse information projection*, arXiv:2402.18810v4, Theorems 3.6, 3.13 and 4.7, characterize effective-null projections and log optimality relative to the projection. In particular, Theorem 4.7 considers suitable mixtures with that projection when it is absent from the original family. Its lower envelope and closure arguments are related. Effective-null invariance under enlarging the mixture class only identifies the numeraire or projection. It does not by itself control the fixed-reference extended integral over members of the original class or provide a finite-gain member. [Primary author text](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18810v4).
- Ashwin Ram, Martin Larsson, Johannes Ruf and Aaditya Ramdas, *Strong duality for the GROW criterion*, arXiv:2606.24768v2, Theorem 3.1, gives a bounded-e-variable minimax equality with a weak-* closed entropy problem, including infinite entropy. The class and reference measure in that optimization differ from the present description-gain objective. Equality for a difference of two finite KL divergences does not give the arbitrary extended log-ratio integral when the divergences can both be infinite. The joint-projection results likewise introduce separate hypotheses. [Primary author text](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24768v2).

## Contribution

The norm closure is already Choquet-closed by standard Hahn–Banach separation and the integration identity for bounded measurable functions. The main step is a geometric countable mixture of approximants that controls the log loss by the last bad index, which has finite expectation even if none of the input approximants has finite gain. The proof gives an explicit approximation inequality on the entire norm closure and preserves a positive-infinite target gain. It includes the distinct obstruction from countably supported laws when the class lacks a common σ-finite dominator. The theorem does not assert attainment of the supremum or general invariance when only (P) and (Q) are dominated.

The search used the exact conjecture wording, author updates and the thesis, later reverse-projection and GROW papers, and KL/log-loss closure terminology. It located no earlier exact theorem or immediate full-scope consequence. No absolute claim of priority is made.
